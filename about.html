About Brock

I care about the work more than the title.

I did not set out to become a technical writer. I did not even know the profession existed until a career counselor listened to my interests in computers, writing, history, and problem solving and asked a simple question: "Have you ever considered technical writing?" My answer was, "What's that?"

That question changed my life. After being rejected from my first technical writing opportunity because I lacked FrameMaker experience, I took a training course, interviewed again, and got the job. I never looked back.

Since then, I've spent more than two decades creating, rescuing, and modernizing documentation for software companies. My career has moved through many of the major shifts in technical communication: FrameMaker, structured authoring, DITA, RoboHelp, Oxygen, MadCap Flare, Git, Docs-as-Code, and now AI-assisted documentation workflows.

Outside of documentation

My interest in systems started long before my career. As a kid I built model airplanes, helicopters, and ships, drew maps for Dungeons & Dragons, studied road atlases, took apart radios just to see the parts, and learned computers on a Commodore VIC-20. I've been an avid reader for as long as I can remember, especially history, horror, and science fiction.

Looking back, I can see the same curiosity that eventually led me into technical documentation: understanding complex things, organizing them, and making them easier for other people to navigate.

How I work

I do not have a separate "work me" and "home me." I am the same person whether I am working, walking, reading, fixing a home network, or having drinks with friends. My best work happens in high-trust environments where people are given room to solve meaningful problems, collaborate when needed, and use deep-focus time to build something that lasts.
