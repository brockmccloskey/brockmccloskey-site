How I Work

This is the practical side of the philosophy.

  1. I try to understand the system before changing it. Products, documentation sets, publishing workflows, and teams all have history. I like to learn how things fit together before I start pulling pieces apart.
  2. I prefer fixing root causes over patching symptoms. A broken topic may need an edit. A pattern of broken topics usually means the architecture, workflow, or source material needs attention.
  3. I care about the customer and the writer. Documentation has to help customers find answers, but it also has to be maintainable by the people responsible for keeping it accurate.
  4. I do not chase tools because they are new. Flare, Git, CSS, AI, Markdown, and Docs-as-Code are useful when they solve real documentation problems.
  5. I use AI as a helper, not a replacement for judgment. AI can reduce repetitive work and speed up analysis, but accuracy, context, and final review still matter.
  6. I like high-trust work. My best work happens when people care about outcomes, give each other room to think, and collaborate when collaboration is actually needed.
